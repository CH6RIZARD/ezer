# Spend the 900 ms braking, not flying

The reveal looks elite when it is mostly deceleration. The dove enters already at speed, flies one short curved path of about 1.5–2 wordmark widths, spends the last third of the timeline braking with a real nose-up flare, and lands on the exact silhouette of the static dove mark at the top right of the R. Then it holds still. Nothing else moves: the type only fades (0.3 → 1, ease-in-out), there are no particles, no glow trail, no camera move, no bounce. In practice this means rendering a rigged white dove in Blender on a Follow Path constraint, viewed through an orthographic camera mapped 1:1 to the wordmark layout, with transparent 8-bit PNG output. Type stays in 2D and is used in Blender only as a holdout occluder. The lighting differs by theme: a cool-grey shadow side and a contact shadow on cream, a warm off-white with a faint rim on purple and black. The quietest flight path ("High glide-in", entirely above the word) is the safest premium choice. "Underline swoop up" is the most expressive one that still reads cleanly. For the wordmark, Bodoni Moda is the strongest fit with EZER's gold-and-Instrument-Serif world, with Archivo Expanded as the safe fintech option. All four recommended fonts are OFL, so they can be used in a logo and bundled in the app. The specification has one tension: a full 3D flight packed into 900 ms sits right at the edge of reading as a "white streak" on dark themes, so path length and peak speed are the parameters to protect.

## Real pigeons brake in their final 300 ms, so the timeline should too

Two facts from bird biomechanics anchor the timing. Pigeon wingbeat frequency runs **6.1–9.6 Hz** and barely changes with climb or descent angle ([Berg & Biewener 2008](https://biewenerlab.oeb.harvard.edu/publications/kinematics-and-power-requirements-ascending-and-descending-flight-pigeon)). Pigeon landings also **peak in deceleration during the last 300 ms** ([Green & Cheng 1998](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/variation-in-kinematics-and-dynamics-of-the-landing-flights-of-pi/)). At real rates, 900 ms contains 6–8 wingbeats, which on a phone-sized mark reads as flicker. So the motion should stylise the measured pattern rather than copy it. Wingbeat amplitude and frequency rise during landing until the very last beat, which drops ([Berg & Biewener 2010 via SICB](https://sicb.org/?p=26922)). Body angle rises by **more than 50°**, the stroke plane tilts up to 30° to push air forward, and the body, tail and wing plane go from near-horizontal to near-vertical ([SICB: Landing flight in the pigeon](https://sicb.org/?p=29175); [Biewener Lab](https://biewenerlab.oeb.harvard.edu/publications/wing-and-body-kinematics-takeoff-and-landing-flight-pigeon-columba-livia)). The tail presses down as a brake ([Fisher 1946](https://sora.unm.edu/sites/default/files/journals/wilson/v071n03/p0267-p0273.pdf)). Raptor studies show the same strategy: brake *before* the perch by diving, then swooping up into a stall within grabbing distance ([Oxford ORA thesis](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:a439bd30-3916-4fe9-ba06-c65fd1547048)). A dove that arrives level, at constant speed and with a closed tail looks pasted on, however good the feathers are.

The research proposed two slightly different splits. One used 450 ms of travel and the other 600 ms. The table below reconciles them around the measured 300 ms braking window. At 60 fps the reveal is 54 frames.

| Window | Frames @60 | Dove | Wordmark |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–80 ms | 1–5 | Already in flight, crossing the frame edge at speed. No take-off, no ease-in | 0.30 → ~0.32 |
| 80–550 ms | 5–33 | One arc, 2–3 big beats on climbs, wings held in glide through turns, bank follows curvature | fading |
| 550–800 ms | 33–48 | Flare: pitch nose-up 50–80°, wings swing forward, 2–3 braking beats with the last one smaller, tail fanned down, feet reach forward | ≈0.94 by 650 ms |
| 800–900 ms | 48–54 | Contact (2–4% body dip, head arrives first), two-stage wing fold, single tail flick, then stillness. Cross-fade into the static dove mark over the last 80–120 ms | → 1.0 |

The wordmark figure is arithmetic, not a source: an ease-in-out cubic fade is 91% complete at 650 ms (0.3 + 0.7 × 0.91 ≈ 0.94). So the spec's own fade already has the type "there" before touchdown, and the landing becomes the final beat. Keep the fade and the flight as **separate tracks**. Drive the flight's path progress with a decelerate-only curve, either Material 3's emphasized decelerate `cubic-bezier(0.05, 0.7, 0.1, 1.0)` ([Flutter Easing.emphasizedDecelerate](https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/material/Easing/emphasizedDecelerate-constant.html)) or the `0.23,1,0.32,1` easeOut EZER already uses in `theme/type.ts`. The final third of the time should cover only the last 10–15% of path length. The "settle" comes from wings, tail and head (secondary motion), never from the position overshooting the perch. A community brand-motion guide places luxury idents at roughly **500–800 ms with "minimal motion, subtle easing, no bounce"** ([dylantarre brand-marketing principles](https://skills.cat/skills/dylantarre/animation-principles/brand-marketing)). That guide is not measured brand data: no published motion specs exist for Rolex, Cartier, Hermès, Amex or Stripe, and Stripe's internal timing tokens are private ([Stripe careers](https://stripe.com/jobs/listing/motion-designer-stripe-identity/7769564)).

The "white streak" comes from distance per frame, not from the colour. No source gives a threshold. A working heuristic is that a white shape travelling more than **half its body length per frame** at 60 fps smears into a flash, worst on #151021 and black, where contrast peaks. If it streaks, shorten the path or lower the peak speed before reaching for blur. Generous blur hurts readability ([ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/motion_blur/)), so confine motion blur or a smear to the one or two fastest frames and keep the bird crisp from the flare onward. Realistic wing animation needs the head leading, the tail reacting last, a broad downstroke, a tight upstroke and asymmetric wings ([Animation Mentor](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/tutorial-how-to-animate-birds-in-flight/)).

## Restraint and theme-specific light do most of the "elite" work

Stock "elegant logo reveal" templates rely on gold particles, shine and smoke ([Motion Array](https://motionarray.com/after-effects-templates/elegant-particle-logo-34471/); [Envato](https://elements.envato.com/gold-and-silver-logo-reveal-ASCUE36)). Those tropes are exactly what marks a reveal as bought rather than designed. Cut drifting feathers, glow trails, touchdown sparkles, camera push-ins, wordmark scale or overshoot, per-letter stagger, multiple light sweeps and second loops. Keep one hero (the dove), one support (the fade) and at most one accent: a contact shadow *or* a single faint sheen tracking just behind the bird, not both at full strength. One depth trick earns its place. If the bird passes **behind one letter once** (holdout-masked) and then lands in front, the type gains a plane and the bird a path through space. Two pass-behinds in 900 ms read as busy. The dove deVOL Kitchens drew from filmed reference (about 60 traced drawings) and its nudge of a letter is the nearest real precedent for a bird physically interacting with a wordmark ([deVOL](https://www.devolkitchens.com/blog/?p=18068)).

The landing hand-off is the single most important continuity detail. The live bird must finish on the exact position, heading and silhouette of the static dove mark and then cross-fade into it, or the screen briefly shows two doves. Apple's guidance has a related trap. The OS launch screen should be nearly identical to the first app screen, because elements that change when launch completes cause a flash ([Apple HIG: Launching](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/launching)). The static splash should therefore show the reveal's **first** frame: the wordmark at 0.3 and an empty perch. Under the OS Reduce Motion setting, skip the flight and fade the wordmark with the dove already perched, using the existing `useReduceMotion()`. The app must never wait on the animation. If loading finishes first, jump to the final state.

A white bird misbehaves differently on each background. deVOL found the opposite case: their dark dove on white read as a blackbird until they inverted it ([deVOL](https://www.devolkitchens.com/blog/?p=18068)). The values below come from the research's design reasoning and are starting points to test on device, not sourced constants.

| Theme | Problem | Feather / light treatment | Depth cue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cream #F7F3EA | Almost no luminance contrast; the bird disappears | Pure-white highlights, cool-grey shadow side (#CFCBC4 → #B9B4AC). No rim light, which vanishes on cream | Soft contact shadow at the perch, about 6–10% black |
| Purple #151021 | Too much contrast; pure white vibrates | Warm off-white feathers (~#F3EFE8), faint lavender or cool rim on the back edge. Any sweep can be a warm gold lift | No shadow (invisible); rely on the one occlusion |
| Black | Worst streak and after-image, especially on OLED | Same as purple, but cap the brightest feather below pure white | Lower peak speed, or blur only on the fastest frames |

In practice this means two lighting variants rather than three: a light-theme render with the shadow-catcher shadow, and a dark-theme render with the rim and a capped white that serves both purple and black.

## The Blender pipeline: path on the object, type as holdout, Blender 5 API traps

**Motion.** Put a Follow Path constraint on the **armature object**, not on a bone, with Follow Curve on. "If this option is not activated, the owner's rotation is not modified by the curve" ([Blender Manual 4.5, Follow Path](https://docs.blender.org/manual/nb/4.5/animation/constraints/relationship/follow_path.html)). Constraining a bone runs into a known bake bug ([#61985](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/issues/61985)). Drive progress with Fixed Position on and a keyed `offset_factor` 0 → 1, which is normalised and script-friendly. Alternatively, key the curve's `eval_time` up to a non-zero `path_duration`; zero values break Animate Path ([T55933](https://developer.blender.org/T55933)). Shape the speed with the F-curve: no ease at the first key, a hard ease-out into the last. The curve's final control point *is* the perch, at the feet/root of the rig. Make the last tangent near-horizontal and coming slightly from above so Follow Curve levels the bird at touchdown. Bank by tilting control points (Ctrl+T) in proportion to curvature, clamp to about ±40°, and set tilt to zero on the last two points. Sources conflict on whether Follow Path honours tilt ([works](https://blenderartists.org/t/making-a-camera-follow-a-path-why-so-difficult/1272829) vs [does not](https://blenderartists.org/t/can-an-object-mesh-with-a-follow-path-constraint-copy-the-tilt-of-a-curve/1147159)), so test it in your build and fall back to a child bank bone if it fails. Layer the flare's nose-up pitch on a child body bone rather than fighting the path orientation. When the motion is approved, bake with **Visual Keying**, which "bakes in the actual transformation values based on the position after the constraint is applied" ([BlenderArtists](https://blenderartists.org/t/how-do-you-bake-out-animation-on-a-constrained-object/1521738)).

**Wings.** Build separate actions for flap, glide, flare, land_fold and perch, and sequence them as NLA strips with blend-in/out. Overlap the shoulder → forearm → hand keys by 1–2 frames each, and loop the flap with a `CYCLES` F-curve modifier ([bpy FCurveModifiers](https://www.docs.blender.org/api/2.80/bpy.types.FCurveModifiers.html)). Change wingbeat rate with the strip `scale`, or key `strip_time` so the rate tracks the path speed ([bpy NlaStrip](https://docs.blender.org/api/2.75/bpy.types.NlaStrip.html)). At 60 fps a 7.5 Hz beat is 8 frames. Key touchdown to the frame where `offset_factor` reaches 1.0. Free starting points: Rigify's bird metarig ([Rig a Pigeon with Rigify](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5p6Rrp135AI)) or a commercially licensed pre-rigged white dove with fly and glide loops ([Superhive, $119](https://superhivemarket.com/products/realistic-animated-white-dove-3d-model)).

**Camera and render.** Use an orthographic camera facing a layout plane. Set `ortho_scale` to W/100 so one world unit equals 100 px, and the designer's SVG coordinates convert directly (`world = (px − W/2)/100`). Depth then never changes size, which lets the bird move in front of or behind the letters freely. The cost is the "toward camera" scale cue: fake the 0.85×–1.15× depth scaling in paths B and D by keying object scale. Turn on Film > Transparent ([Manual 5.2 Film](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/cycles/render_settings/film.html)). Motion blur at shutter 0.5 still works on the moving bird. The documented ortho limitation applies to *camera* blur only ([Manual 5.2 Motion Blur](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/cycles/render_settings/motion_blur.html)). Render the bird in **Cycles on CPU**. It has a real shadow catcher that writes the contact shadow into the alpha ([Manual 5.2 Object Settings](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/cycles/object_settings/object_data.html)). EEVEE has none ([BlenderArtists](https://blenderartists.org/t/shadow-catcher-in-eevee-next-blender-4-2/1548135)) and needs a GPU context even with `-b` ([devtalk](https://devtalk.blender.org/t/blender-2-8-unable-to-open-a-display-by-the-rendering-on-the-background-eevee/1436/19)). Use the **Standard** view transform so whites can reach true white. AgX deliberately rolls whites off toward grey ([EaryChow/AgX](https://github.com/EaryChow/AgX)). Whichever you pick, keep it identical across variants. Feathers: Principled BSDF with base about 0.85–0.9 grey-white, roughness 0.5–0.6, a little Sheen and a touch of Subsurface; light with a large soft key from upper-left, a cool fill and a rim on the dark variant. Output **8-bit PNG RGBA**. Blender writes straight alpha there, and 16-bit PNG has a history of premultiply bugs ([#99186](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/issues/99186)).

**Type.** Typeset EZER in 2D as outlined vector, the brand-exact artwork. In Blender, load the same letters (Text object with the same font, or the imported SVG) aligned through the same px↔world mapping, and give them a **Holdout** material, which creates "a 'hole' in the image with zero alpha" ([Manual 5.2 Holdout](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/shader_nodes/shader/holdout.html)). Place the occluder in front of the bird only where the bird should pass behind, and turn off its shadow visibility. The composite is then the 2D wordmark with the bird render (holes already cut) on top.

**Blender 5.x changes that break older scripts** ([5.0 Python API notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/5.0/python_api/)):
- `action.fcurves`, `action.groups` and `action.id_root` are removed. Reach F-curves through channelbags (`strip.channelbag(slot, ensure=True)` ([ActionKeyframeStrip](https://docs.blender.org/api/5.0/bpy.types.ActionKeyframeStrip.html))), and `id_root` becomes `action_slot.target_id_type`.
- The EEVEE engine id is now `BLENDER_EEVEE`, no longer `BLENDER_EEVEE_NEXT`.
- `ImageFormatSettings.media_type` must be set *before* `file_format`.
- `scene.node_tree` becomes `scene.compositing_node_group`.
- Several render-pass and EEVEE AO property names changed.

Blender 5.0 also reworked colour management, adding wide-gamut, ACES and AgX HDR views ([5.0 EEVEE notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/5.0/eevee/)). Pin the view transform by name in the script. Run headless with `blender -b dove.blend --python build.py -- --variant B`, and keep `-a` after the script because arguments are processed left to right ([Manual: command line](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/3.0/advanced/command_line/arguments.html)). Render-time figures (roughly 15–60 s per Cycles frame on a laptop CPU) are unverified estimates. Benchmark three frames first. The 54-frame shot is short, so even the slow end is under an hour per variant.

**Delivery into the app.** The earlier method study chose an in-place flap sprite moved by code along the path, because it keeps the landing pixel-exact at any screen size and needs no new native dependency. That reasoning still holds. But a side-view sprite cannot show the bank, the 50°+ flare pitch or the depth crossing that make a 900 ms 3D landing believable. The clean synthesis is to render the *whole flight* in Blender on a transparent canvas whose coordinate frame **is the wordmark's box**. The app then scales that canvas uniformly with the wordmark, so the landing stays exact. One progress value picks the frame. 54 frames of a canvas wider than the wordmark will not fit one 2048 px texture at full resolution, so either deliver at 30 fps (27 frames) or split the frames across two or three sheets. This trade-off is reasoned, not measured. Check decode time and `gfxinfo` on the S22 before committing. The earlier study's ~3.5 s timing is superseded by this 900 ms spec.

## Four flight paths, from quietest to most theatrical

The coordinates come from the flight-path research. x runs from 0 at the left edge of the first E to 1 at the right edge of the R. y runs from 0 at cap top to 1 at the baseline, with negative values above the word. The perch is **(1.17, −0.59)**; move it to the real dove-mark position once the font is chosen. Run the points through a centripetal Catmull-Rom spline, or hand-tune Béziers with aligned, length-proportional handles. G2 continuity, the clothoid logic roads and roller coasters use so that turning builds smoothly ([Illinois Dynamics Reference](https://dynref.engr.illinois.edu/avt.html)), keeps the bank angle from popping. All four enter from the left and finish rising toward the top right, so the reading direction and the flight direction agree ([Austin Kleon on Van Sijll](https://tumblr.austinkleon.com/tagged/direction)). The length column is the polyline length in wordmark widths, assuming cap height is 25–30% of the wordmark width. It is a quick check against the streak budget, not a measured value.

| Path | Control points (x, y) | Length (widths) | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B. High glide-in** | (−0.30,−1.70) → (0.15,−1.30) → (0.55,−1.02) → (0.92,−0.72) → (1.08,−0.52) → (1.17,−0.59); scale 0.85 → 1.0 | ~1.5 | Quietest and most premium. One descending arc, never crosses the letters, with a small dip below the perch and a rise into it. Matches the straighter "familiar perch" approach |
| **A. Underline swoop up** | (−0.30,1.55) → (0.10,1.42) → (0.50,1.48) → (0.85,1.25) → (1.08,0.60) → (1.16,−0.20) → (1.17,−0.59) | ~1.8 | Underlines the whole name, then the climb up past the R converts speed into a natural nose-up flare. Best balance of gesture and legibility |
| **D. Weave through depth** | (−0.15,−0.80)[0.8×] → (0.22,−0.25) → (0.40,0.50)[behind Z, holdout] → (0.62,1.30)[in front, 1.15×] → (0.95,1.20) → (1.22,0.55) → (1.24,−0.20) → (1.17,−0.59)[1.0×] | ~2.0 | One S-curve with one depth crossing. Feels three-dimensional. Needs the holdout setup |
| **C. Circle the mark** | (−0.30,0.45) → (0.30,0.25) → (0.80,−0.05) → (1.25,−0.25) → (1.40,−0.60) → (1.27,−0.90) → (1.05,−0.85) → (1.00,−0.62) → (1.08,−0.52) → (1.17,−0.59) | ~2.4 | One clockwise orbit in empty space to the right of the R points the eye at the perch. Longest path, so the highest streak risk |

The motion-craft and flight-path research disagree on the length budget: about 1–1.5 widths versus 2.5–3.5 units. Measured in widths, the proposals land between the two. **B and A sit comfortably inside a 900 ms budget. D and C are at the edge**, and on black they will need either the lower-speed tweak or the single-frame blur. All four are simpler than the client's hand-drawn loop-drop-undersweep-rise path, which the research judged to be at the busy end for 900 ms. Within that time, about 1.5 turns is the readable ceiling. Keep any loop out of the band between cap line and baseline so the word stays legible ([UIC 12 Principles on arcs](https://www.evl.uic.edu/datsoupi/251/docs/12AnimationPrinciples.pdf)). Whichever path you choose, the final approach heading must match the facing of the static dove mark.

## Four OFL fonts for EZER, ranked for a dove on the R

All four are SIL OFL 1.1 on Google Fonts. The OFL FAQ says using them in a logo is explicitly allowed ("Yes. You are very welcome to do so."), the artwork does not become OFL, and bundling in a commercial app is fine if you ship the licence text ([OFL FAQ](https://openfontlicense.org/ofl-faq/)). That settles the question of hand-modifying glyphs (a custom R leg, a dove notch), which Fontshare's ITF licence leaves murky ([licenseorg](https://licenseorg.com/guide/fonts/fontshare)). The weights and tracking are design judgement built on Pimp my Type's rule: start all caps at **+0.1em** and open until dense spots clear, but stop before another letter could fit between two. Light weights and sans need more, bold and serif less ([Pimp my Type](https://pimpmytype.com/spacing-all-caps/)). Then kern each pair by eye in 5–10 unit steps ([Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/kerning-tracking-leading/)).

| Rank | Font | Direction | Hero weight | Tracking | Why / watch for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Bodoni Moda** ([Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Bodoni+Moda/about)) | High-contrast Didone, "couture" | 500, max optical size | +0.12 to +0.18em | The Vogue and Bazaar vocabulary ([Inkbot](https://inkbotdesign.com/vogue-logo/)). It sits naturally with Instrument Serif and gold on #151021. The Z's thick diagonal turns heavy (thin it 3–5%). Hairlines vanish at app-bar sizes, so make a heavier small master. Confirm the LICENSE in the repo |
| 2 | **Archivo**, wdth Expanded ([Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Archivo)) | Wide grotesk, "private bank / card issuer" | 400–500 | +0.20 to +0.30em | Most legible at 14 px, and the widest R gives the dove the most room. Risk: it is the "blanding" look luxury is leaving ([Monocle](https://monocle.com/design/serif-revival-fashion-brand-logos/)), so the dove and the gold carry all the distinctiveness |
| 3 | **Jost** ([Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/jost-vs-futura/)) | Futura-like geometric, "maison / gallery" | 300–400 hero, 500 small | +0.25 to +0.40em | The semicircular R bowl gives the cleanest tangent perch, the most logo-like integration. Below 300 it vanishes on phones |
| 4 | **Cinzel** ([CTAN](https://ctan.org/tex-archive/fonts/cinzel?lang=en)) | Roman inscriptional caps | 500–600 | +0.14 to +0.20em | The classical dove and Hebrew "Ezer" share its register, and it has the most expressive R. But it is the free Trajan, the film-poster face ([Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/cinzel-vs-trajan/)), and its Z is a later invention that often needs redrawing |

For all four, draw the dove at the font's **thinnest stroke weight**, at 30–40% of cap height, with its top at or a hair above cap height and a gap from the R bowl at least one hairline wide, so it reads as perched rather than fused. The pairs that matter in EZER are Z–E (opens at the bottom), E–Z (tighten), and E–R (two stems, may need a touch more). E·Z·E is palindromic, so match E–Z and Z–E optically. The dove adds weight at the top right, so nudge the lockup left to centre it optically. Ship the wordmark as outlined SVG with tracking baked in. Trend context favours a serif: luxury has been reverting from the 2017–19 sans wave to heritage serifs and emblems (Burberry's knight is the "word + small emblem" model) ([Monocle](https://monocle.com/design/serif-revival-fashion-brand-logos/)), while fintech stays grotesk-heavy ([Monzo](https://monzo.com/blog/weve-had-a-little-makeover)). That makes Bodoni Moda the differentiating choice and Archivo the conventional one.

## Conclusion

The research reframes "elegant" as a physics problem as much as a taste problem. The same constraint that governs real pigeons, braking concentrated in the final 300 ms, is what makes the reveal read as calm and expensive, and the same constraint that limits motion blur, distance per frame, is what separates a dove from a white streak. That leaves path length and the landing hand-off as the two parameters to guard. Feathers, fonts and lighting can all be iterated afterwards without re-architecting anything.

Two things remain unmeasured and should be settled on the phone before polishing anything else. The first is whether the longer paths C and D stay legible on black at 60 fps. The second is whether the full-flight frame sequence decodes fast enough on a low-end Android to start on time. Resolve both with a rough grey-box render of path B first. If that one feels premium, the rest of the work is finish.
